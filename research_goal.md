## Background

Recent decades have experienced substantial changes in tropical precipitation and atmospheric circulation. Understanding the mechanisms behind these changes is important for determining how anthropogenic climate change is affecting the tropical climate system and for evaluating the reliability of climate models.

The 2026 *Nature Communications* paper investigates the question:

> **To what extent are recent tropical precipitation changes driven by thermodynamic effects versus changes in atmospheric circulation, and what physical mechanisms explain these changes?**

---

## What the Primary Paper Studies

The paper analyzes tropical precipitation changes over recent decades, with particular attention to the role of large-scale atmospheric circulation.

It shows that changes in precipitation cannot be explained by thermodynamic warming alone. Changes in atmospheric circulation play an important role, particularly changes associated with:

* land–sea thermal contrast,
* Indo-Pacific sea surface temperature patterns,
* Walker circulation,
* ITCZ position and structure,
* and large-scale tropical atmospheric circulation.

The study also compares observed/reanalysis changes with simulations from multiple CMIP6 climate models.

A major finding is that the observed recent tropical precipitation response is not fully reproduced by the CMIP6 models.

The study further uses climate-model sensitivity experiments to investigate the physical role of land–sea thermal contrast in producing changes in atmospheric circulation and precipitation.

## Datasets

The study uses several publicly available climate datasets.

### ERA5

ERA5 reanalysis provides the primary atmospheric and surface climate fields used to characterize the observed/reanalysis state of the climate system.

Relevant variables include precipitation, surface temperature, sea surface temperature, atmospheric circulation, humidity, and other atmospheric variables.

### GPCP

The Global Precipitation Climatology Project (GPCP) dataset provides an independent precipitation dataset for comparison with ERA5.

### Berkeley Earth

Berkeley Earth surface temperature data provide an independent observational estimate of surface temperature changes.

### NOAA OISST

OISST provides an independent sea surface temperature dataset used to evaluate observed SST changes.

### NCEP/DOE Reanalysis 2

NCEP/DOE Reanalysis 2 provides an additional reanalysis dataset for evaluating atmospheric circulation changes.

### CMIP6

The paper uses simulations from multiple CMIP6 climate models to evaluate how well climate models reproduce the observed recent tropical climate response.

The analysis includes variables such as precipitation, surface temperature, and sea surface temperature, together with additional atmospheric information where relevant.

---

## What the Paper Resolves

The paper provides evidence that recent tropical precipitation changes are strongly associated with changes in atmospheric circulation and land–sea thermal contrast.

It therefore moves beyond a simple thermodynamic explanation of:

> global warming → more atmospheric moisture → precipitation change

and demonstrates the importance of the dynamical response of the climate system.

The study also identifies a substantial discrepancy between the observed/reanalysis response and the CMIP6 model response.

In other words, the paper establishes that:

> **Recent observed tropical precipitation changes and the corresponding circulation changes are not fully captured by the CMIP6 model ensemble.**

---

## What Remains Unresolved

Although the paper identifies substantial differences between observations and CMIP6 simulations, the CMIP6 ensemble is composed of many individual climate models with substantially different representations of the tropical climate system.

The models differ in their simulation of:

* Indo-Pacific SST patterns,
* Pacific SST gradients,
* land–sea thermal contrast,
* tropical atmospheric circulation,
* Walker circulation,
* ITCZ structure,
* and tropical precipitation.

The multi-model ensemble provides an overall picture of model behavior, but it does not fully explain **why individual models differ from one another**.

This leaves an important unresolved question:

> **Why are some CMIP6 models better able to reproduce the observed recent tropical precipitation and circulation response than others?**

More specifically:

> **Which differences among individual climate models are responsible for the inter-model spread in the simulated tropical precipitation response?**

---

## Research Gap

The existing study establishes:

> **Observed tropical precipitation changes are strongly connected to changes in atmospheric circulation and land–sea thermal contrast.**

It also establishes:

> **CMIP6 models do not fully reproduce the observed response.**

However, it does not fully establish:

> **What distinguishes individual models that reproduce the observed response from models that do not.**

The unresolved problem is therefore not simply whether CMIP6 models disagree.

It is:

> **What causes the disagreement between individual climate models in their simulation of recent tropical precipitation change?**

This is the central research gap motivating the present work.
